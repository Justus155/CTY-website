CTY's public safeguarding commitment for children, teens and young people who interact with the ministry online or in person.

Safeguarding is part of ministry

CTY Ministries serves children, teens and young people. Safeguarding is therefore not an optional website feature—it is part of responsible ministry, and part of what it means to be trusted with the next generation of this church family.

"Defend the weak and the fatherless; uphold the cause of the poor and the oppressed."

Psalm 82:3

Core principle: children and young people should never be treated as ordinary marketing material. Their dignity, privacy and safety come first.

Screening, vetting & code of conduct

Anyone who serves in a role with regular access to children, teens or youth should complete CTY's screening process before starting, which may include an application, references and a background check appropriate to the role and applicable local law.

Staff and volunteers working with minors are expected to follow a written code of conduct, including practices such as having more than one adult present during activities with minors, keeping communication with minors appropriately visible to other leaders or parents, and avoiding private one-to-one contact outside of ministry settings.

  • Screening and, where applicable, background checks are completed before unsupervised access to minors begins.
  • Leaders and volunteers receive safeguarding orientation appropriate to their role.
  • Two or more authorised adults are present for activities involving minors wherever reasonably possible.

Online interactions

Website forms and digital communication should be designed to reduce opportunities for inappropriate one-to-one contact with minors. Where communication with children or young people is necessary, CTY should follow its approved safeguarding procedures and use appropriate channels and adult oversight.

Personal information

CTY should avoid collecting unnecessary personal information from children and young people. Forms should clearly explain why information is needed, who may receive it and how it will be handled.

Photography & media

Photos and videos involving children or teens should be handled through CTY's media and consent procedures. Avoid publishing information that could unnecessarily identify, locate or expose a child to risk.

For the detailed media workflow, see Media & Photo Consent.

Reporting a concern

If you have a safeguarding concern, do not attempt to investigate it yourself or confront a child about it. Report it promptly through CTY's designated safeguarding process.

"Speak up for those who cannot speak for themselves, for the rights of all who are destitute."

Proverbs 31:8

Safeguarding contact: safeguarding@ctyministries.org, or speak directly to a CTY pastor or designated safeguarding leader.

Immediate danger? If there is an immediate danger or emergency, contact local emergency services or your area's child-protection authority right away — do not wait on a response from CTY first.

Handling concerns

Reports should be treated seriously, confidentially and on a need-to-know basis, while allowing appropriate escalation where safety or law requires it. CTY should document concerns and follow its safeguarding procedures rather than relying on informal promises of secrecy.

Speaking up

People should be able to raise genuine safeguarding concerns without intimidation or retaliation. Deliberately making false reports can be harmful, but uncertainty should not prevent someone from reporting a concern in good faith.

Training & review

Safeguarding procedures should be reviewed regularly and supported by appropriate training for people who work with children, young people, communications, events, photography or online systems.

This webpage should complement—not replace—CTY's full internal safeguarding procedures.
